⚙️ Part 1.5: Game Development Technology

How Technology Powers Game Design

Game development uses programming, art, sound, and AI working together.

Technology brings your game ideas to life!

🎮 1. Game Engines: The Core Technology

A game engine is the backbone of game development. It provides:

Graphics Rendering

Converts 2D and 3D models into real-time visuals

Physics Simulation

Handles gravity, collision, and object interactions

AI & Scripting

Controls mechanics and NPC behaviors

Popular Game Engines

🔷

Unity

Indie & mobile games, uses C#

⚫

Unreal Engine

AAA games, high-end graphics

🚀

Godot

Open-source alternative

🛠️

Custom Engines

Large studios (Rockstar, CD Projekt)

💻 2. Programming & Scripting Languages

Code brings game mechanics to life:

⚡

C++

AAA games – fast and efficient

#️⃣

C#

Unity scripting for game logic

🐍

Python

AI, automation, backend services

📝

JavaScript & Lua

Web games and lightweight scripting

💡 Uses: Movement, scoring, AI behaviors, puzzle triggers

🎨 3. Graphics & Rendering Technology

Beautiful visuals require specialized tools:

3D Modeling Tools

Blender

Free, open-source 3D modeling

Maya & 3ds Max

AAA studio modeling tools

ZBrush

Detailed character sculpting

Rendering Techniques

Ray Tracing

Realistic lighting & reflections

Shaders

Control how light interacts with surfaces

Particle Effects

Fire, smoke, explosions

2D Graphics: Photoshop (textures), Aseprite (pixel art), Spine (2D animation)

🤖 4. Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Games

AI makes games smarter and more engaging:

Finite State Machines

Simple AI behavior (enemy patrolling)

Pathfinding (A*)

NPCs navigate environments efficiently

Machine Learning

AI adapts to player actions

Procedural Generation

AI creates levels dynamically (No Man's Sky)

💡 Uses: NPC behavior, adaptive difficulty, automated testing

🌐 5. Multiplayer & Online Gaming

Large-scale online games

Peer-to-Peer

Smaller multiplayer games

Cloud Gaming

Tools

Photon, Mirror, AWS GameLift

📚 Part 2: Story and Gameplay

1️⃣ What is Story in a Game?

Story gives meaning to the player's actions.

🍄

Super Mario Bros.

Simple goal: Save the princess

🧟

The Last of Us

Emotional and deep narrative

💡 Key Insight: A good game balances story + gameplay

2️⃣ Types of Interactive Storytelling

📿

String of Pearls

  • • Linear story progression
  • • Small areas of freedom
  • • Main story doesn't change much

🎮 Example: Uncharted 4: A Thief's End

Linear progression to goal

🌐

Story Machine (Emergent)

  • • Player creates their own story
  • • Open world or sandbox style
  • • High replayability

🎮 Examples: GTA V, The Sims 4

Multiple paths, player choice

3️⃣ ⚠️ Common Problems in Game Storytelling

🔀

Too many endings

Weaker emotional impact

🌳

Too many branches

Excessive development work

💾

Save/Load abuse

Reduces drama and stakes

😶

Limited expression

Characters can't show deep thoughts

📐 Simple Story Formula

GOAL
+
OBSTACLES
+
CONFLICT

🏰 Example: Dungeon Escape

GOAL

Escape the dungeon

OBSTACLES

Traps and monsters

CONFLICT

Player vs Dungeon Boss

🎯 Part 3: Indirect Control

What is Indirect Control?

Guiding players without making them feel forced.

Players feel free — but the designer is secretly guiding them.

🛠️ Methods of Indirect Control

🎯

1. Goals

Clear objectives guide players naturally toward intended actions.

🏎️ Racing games: The finish line directs all movement

👁️

2. Visual Design

Bright lights, open doors, and arrows capture attention.

💡 🚪 ➡️
🎵

3. Music

Sound sets the emotional pace for player behavior.

🔊 Fast = Urgency

🔈 Soft = Calm

👤

4. Characters

Emotional connection drives player behavior and protection instincts.

🧟 The Last of Us: Players protect Ellie because they care

🚧

5. Constraints

Limiting choices makes decisions easier and more meaningful.

100 weapons

❌
→

3 meaningful weapons

✅

⚖️ Ethical Game Design

Game designers have a responsibility to players:

✔️ Respect players
✔️ Avoid manipulation
✔️ Avoid unfair traps
✔️ Create fun, not frustration
